Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions we hear most.

What exactly is a foreclosure surplus?
When a home sells at a foreclosure auction for more than the amount owed on the mortgage plus costs and fees, the difference is the "surplus." State law says that leftover money belongs to the former homeowner — but most people never know it exists, and it often goes unclaimed.
How much does this cost me?
Nothing upfront. Our fee is 30% of the funds we recover on your behalf — and only if money is actually recovered. If we don't recover anything, you owe us nothing.
How long does it take?
Timelines vary by state and by whether the surplus is held by the court or the county. Many claims can be filed within a few weeks of signing; payment timing depends on the processing office. We track your claim through every step and keep you updated.
Is this legal?
Yes. Unclaimed surplus funds are legally the property of the former homeowner, and every state provides a process to claim them. We follow each state's rules and generate state-specific claim forms — and we encourage you to review anything with a lawyer of your choosing before signing.
What if I already filed a claim myself?
If you've already filed your own claim, you don't need us — congratulations. If your claim is still pending or you're not sure, our team can help you understand where things stand.
