What the template covers

  • Your Information — your name and street address, plus optional date of birth, Social Security number, email, and phone
  • The Credit Reporting Agency (the Credit Bureau) — the bureau's name and mailing address. Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion are named inside that blank as a factual example; nothing in the template recommends one
  • Date and Reference — the date you send it, and optionally your file number and the date of the report you are disputing
  • The Item I Am Disputing — the creditor or company name and account number exactly as they appear on your report
  • Statement of Dispute — Option A inaccurate, Option B incomplete, Option C not mine, Option D other; each ends in a blank to describe it in your own words
  • Request for Reinvestigation — the request itself, citing the federal statute, with an optional note to remove that citation
  • What I Ask You to Do — your request, written into the letter, plus a request that the bureau send you written notice of the results
  • Supporting Documents — optional, for anything you are enclosing
  • Response Period — a blank where you enter a number of days for the reply
  • Closing and Signature — a single signature block (a letter from one sender, not a two-party agreement)
Plus a one-page “How to use this template” cover and a full disclaimer at the end. Section 5 sets four options; you keep the one that applies and delete the others.
Sections 6 and 7 are requests written into the letter — not promises that a bureau will respond, reinvestigate, or change anything. 4 / 6
