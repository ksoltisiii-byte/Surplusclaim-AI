What the template covers

  • The Parties — names, addresses, and effective date
  • Purpose — where you describe what the information is being shared for
  • Definition of Confidential Information — what counts as covered, plus an optional marking requirement
  • Exclusions from Confidential Information — four standard exclusions, plus room to add another
  • Obligations of the Receiving Party — how the information must be handled, and who it may be shared with
  • Permitted Disclosures Required by Law — notice, cooperation, and limiting what is disclosed
  • Duration — how many years the obligations run and from which date, plus optional trade-secret and survival wording
  • Return or Destruction of Materials — including optional certification and retention language
  • No License and No Warranty — ownership stays with the disclosing party, and the information is provided “as is”
  • One-Way or Mutual — Option A (one-way) and Option B (mutual), choose one
  • Remedies — optional, with a prompt to describe what the parties want and an optional limitation
  • Governing Law — the state the parties choose, plus optional venue or dispute-resolution wording
  • General — entire agreement, amendments, severability, no waiver, and optional assignment and counterparts wording
  • Notices — addresses and emails
  • Signatures — sign-off blocks for both parties
  • Both options, one file — because Section 10 sits inside the document, you don’t buy the one-way version and the mutual version separately.
Plus a one-page “How to use this template” cover and a full disclaimer at the end. Both parties are named in the document — it can be filled in from either point of view.
