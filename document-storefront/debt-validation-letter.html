Debt Validation Letter

Blank template · Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §1692g(b) · $19.99, one-time download

What this template is for

A debt collector has written to you about a debt you are not sure you owe — or believe is wrong. Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, if you dispute a debt in writing within 30 days of the collector's first written notice, the collector must stop collection until it sends you verification of the debt. This blank letter makes that request.

You decide whether this template fits your situation and fill it in yourself. We do not advise you to send it and we do not complete it for you.

The blank template

Copy the text below into your own document or write it out by hand, then fill in the blanks — [in brackets] — yourself. Nothing is prefilled for you.

[Your name] [Your address] [City, State, ZIP] [Date] [Debt collector name] [Debt collector address] Re: Account # [number from their letter] To whom it may concern: I am responding to your letter dated [date] about the debt you claim I owe. I dispute this debt and request that you validate it. Please provide: (1) the name and address of the original creditor; (2) the amount you claim is owed, and the basis for it; (3) documentation that you are authorized to collect this debt. I am making this request under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §1692g(b). Sincerely, [Your name]

Honest usage notes

  • The 30-day dispute window runs from the date of the collector's first written notice to you. If you intend to send this letter, send it within that window.
  • Send it by certified mail with a return receipt requested so you have proof it was mailed and delivered.
  • Keep a copy of the letter and the mailing receipt for your own records.
  • Fill in only what the blanks ask for. The template does not request — and you should not volunteer — your full Social Security number, bank account numbers, or other sensitive information in the letter.
  • This letter does not create an obligation on the collector to agree with you. It asks for verification; the collector decides how to respond, and the law governs what happens next. No outcome is guaranteed.

What this does / does NOT do

What it does

  • States, in writing, that you dispute the debt — the step that triggers your right under 15 U.S.C. §1692g(b) to have collection paused until the collector verifies the debt.
  • Asks for the three specific items the law contemplates: the original creditor's identity, the claimed amount and its basis, and proof the collector may collect it.
  • Puts your request in a format a debt collector receives every day.

What it does NOT do

  • It does not erase, reduce, forgive, or pay any debt.
  • It does not guarantee that the collector will stop, that the debt will be validated or invalidated, or that anything on your credit report will change.
  • It is not legal advice, and sending it does not make you a lawyer's client.
  • It is not a credit repair service. No credit repair services are offered.
