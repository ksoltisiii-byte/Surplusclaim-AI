Credit Report Dispute Letter

Blank template · Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. §1681i · $19.99, one-time download

What this template is for

A credit reporting agency (Equifax, Experian, or TransUnion) is showing an item on your report that you believe is inaccurate or incomplete. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, if you dispute an item in writing, the agency must investigate it — generally at no charge to you — and correct or delete it if it cannot be verified. This blank letter makes that request.

You decide whether this template fits your situation and fill it in yourself. We do not advise you to send it and we do not complete it for you.

The blank template

Copy the text below into your own document or write it out by hand, then fill in the blanks — [in brackets] — yourself. Nothing is prefilled for you.

To: Equifax / Experian / TransUnion Re: Dispute of inaccurate information I dispute the following item on my credit report as inaccurate or incomplete: [describe the item]. Please investigate it under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. §1681i, and correct or delete it if it cannot be verified. [Your name, date of birth, last 4 of SSN, current address]

Honest usage notes

  • Send it to the agency that issued the report you are looking at — Equifax, Experian, or TransUnion. Each agency investigates disputes on its own report.
  • Describe the item specifically in the blank, e.g., an account, a balance, a payment status, or an entry you believe is not yours. The more specific you are, the clearer your request is — but we do not write the description for you.
  • Do not send original documents. If you include anything at all (e.g., a statement showing a payment), send copies, never originals.
  • Keep a copy of the letter and, if you mail it, use certified mail with a return receipt requested and keep the receipt.
  • The agency must generally investigate within 30 days (occasionally longer if you provide additional information during the investigation) and must notify you of the results. How it responds in your case is not something we can predict or promise. No outcome is guaranteed.

What this does / does NOT do

What it does

  • Puts your dispute in writing — the step that starts the agency's reinvestigation process under 15 U.S.C. §1681i.
  • Identifies the item you dispute so the agency knows what to look at.
  • Gives the agency the identifiers (your name, date of birth, last 4 of your Social Security number, current address) it needs to find your file.

What it does NOT do

  • It does not erase, remove, or "clean up" anything from your report on its own, and it does not guarantee the item will be corrected or deleted.
  • It does not erase, reduce, or eliminate any debt.
  • It is not legal advice, and sending it does not make you a lawyer's client.
  • It is not a credit repair service. No credit repair services are offered.
